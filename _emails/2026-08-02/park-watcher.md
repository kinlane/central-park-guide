Hi park watchers — three things stand out in the data this week. Naumburg's 121st season closes Tuesday with its August concert, the last of five. Wednesday is genuinely empty — a park-wide blank on the calendar, which happens maybe twice a summer. And the week carries 596 permitted events across seven days with exactly one of them touching a drive, which is an unusually low road load for early August.

## Weather this week
A stormy 81°F Monday, a clear 83°F Tuesday, then a steady climb through 82°F Wednesday and 87°F Thursday into three straight days at 88°F with afternoon thunderstorm chances. The pattern to note is the back half: Friday, Saturday and Sunday are identical on the forecast — hot, humid, mostly sunny with late convection — which is the first stretch of genuinely settled summer heat since the Fourth.

## The season markers
**Naumburg's 121st season ends Tuesday.** Nosky's Baroque Band at 7:30 is the fifth and final concert of the 2026 run, which began June 9 with Baroklyn and Simone Dinnerstein and continued through The Knights, Delirium Musicum and the Orchestra of St. Luke's. The oldest free outdoor classical series in the country closes for another year, and nothing replaces it at the Bandshell until next June.

**SummerStage is at its August peak.** Two free Rumsey shows this week — Andrew Bird with the Wordless Music Orchestra Thursday, Funk Flex's R&B bill Friday — with the umbrella "SummerStage Festival – August" permit holding Rumsey Thursday through Sunday. The series has also grown a self-referential layer this year: the Arsenal is running two free programs about SummerStage's own history, the Vartoogian photography exhibit all week and "Foundational Voices" Thursday afternoon. A concert series curating its own archive at forty is worth noting as a shift.

**Bethesda Terrace and Fountain is in week two of a thirty-two-day closure**, Aug 2 through Sept 2. That is the longest single closure of a marquee landmark on this year's calendar and it reroutes foot traffic through the 72nd Street crossing for the duration.

## The operational picture
**One road permit in seven days.** The CRCA Central Park Racing Series holds East Drive Sunday 4:00–8:30 AM and that is the entire road load — no race, no charity walk, no staged event on the loop otherwise. For an early-August week that is remarkably light; the same week last month carried a half marathon and a 5K/10K on consecutive mornings.

**Recurring weekend closures.** East Meadow is closed Saturday and Sunday, and the pattern repeats every weekend through Aug 30 — nine dated closures in the data. East 106th Street Lawn is closed Monday through Thursday, the Bowling Greens and East 102nd Street Landscape Tuesday, the East 72nd Street Playground Lawn Monday.

**Wednesday is a park-wide blank.** No tours, no concerts, no closures beyond the East 106th lawn, no crowd-drawing permits at any gate. Fifty softball permits and fifteen private bookings, and nothing else. Worth watching whether this holds — the last comparable gap was mid-July.

## The volume, by day
Softball and T-ball run 34 to 71 permits a day, peaking Thursday at 71 across North Meadow, Great Lawn and Heckscher — the three fields carry 351 of the week's permits between them. Private bookings peak Saturday at 31, which is more than double any weekday, and the scenic spots absorb nearly all of them. Markets appear at two gates simultaneously Saturday: Columbus Circle Food Market at Merchants' Gate Thursday through Saturday, Unity Markets at Farmers' Gate Saturday only.

## One to name
**Summer on the Harlem Meer, Sunday 9 to 6 at the Dana Discovery Center**, with Uptown Royalty performing free at 2. The north end's programming has been consistent all summer and the Meer is carrying more free weekend activity than any comparable site south of the Reservoir — a pattern that's held since June and deserves more attention than it gets.

## A data note
**The CRCA series is untagged.** Sunday's East Drive permit carries only a "Family & Community" tag in our data, with no `Affects Loop` flag and no cycling tag, despite being a crit race holding a drive for four and a half hours. Same for the other three CRCA dates this month — Aug 16 and Aug 30. It surfaces correctly in the runner and cyclist digests because we caught it by location, not by tag, which is exactly the failure mode the affects-loop rule exists to prevent.
