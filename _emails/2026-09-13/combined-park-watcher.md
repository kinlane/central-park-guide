*Friday's digest ran through the 18th; the front half will be familiar. Two findings are new, and both are the kind this digest exists for.*

The Bethesda Terrace closure, which I have described three weeks running as unexplained and open-ended, is neither open-ended nor quite as mysterious as it looked: the permits run **contiguously to October 13** and then stop. And the field-permit migration I flagged last week as worth confirming has confirmed itself emphatically — Heckscher is now carrying nearly twice the Great Lawn's load, a reversal of the entire summer.

## Weather this week

Monday clears to 74°, Tuesday through Thursday run sunny and dry at 72–83°, and shower chances build from Friday to 37% next Sunday. Dry midweek weather is what keeps a restoration schedule on its stated dates, so the lawn closures should end when they say.

## Bethesda Terrace: an end date, at last

The all-day permits titled only **"Closed"** now appear in the feed running unbroken from today through **October 13** — 31 consecutive days ahead, no gaps, on top of the roughly two weeks already elapsed. Call it a six-week closure of the park's most photographed structure.

I have offered two readings and hedged between them. The end date settles it. An administrative hold does not run six weeks and does not terminate on a specific Tuesday; scheduled physical work does both. What remains genuinely odd is the silence: six weeks at Bethesda Terrace with no public notice, no Conservancy announcement, and a permit record that says nothing but "Closed." **The work is no longer the question. The absence of any notice about it is.**

## The leagues have moved to Heckscher

Field permits by venue, across four weeks of this digest:

| Venue | Sep 5–11 | Sep 7–13 | Sep 12–18 | Sep 14–20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heckscher Fields | 24 | 34 | ~60 | **92** |
| Great Lawn | ~70 | ~90 | ~80 | **55** |
| North Meadow | 5 | 17 | 24 | **38** |

Last week I called Heckscher's doubling the sharpest single-venue move in the series and said it needed confirming. It has nearly doubled again, and the Great Lawn has fallen by a third in the same week. This is not growth — it is **relocation**. The obvious reading is the Great Lawn going into autumn turf protection while fall leagues shift south to Heckscher and north to North Meadow, which is the pattern you would expect and which I will keep watching for a lawn-closure permit that confirms it.

Total load is 189 permits, up from 182.

## Sun Sep 20, 7 AM–noon — the 18-miler

**[TCS New York City Marathon Training Series 18M](https://events.nyrr.org/tcs-new-york-city-marathon-training-series-18m)** holds the Ted Corbitt Loop for five hours. The marathon build-up is now visibly underway on the calendar; expect the series' remaining legs and then the marathon itself on November 1.

Worth noting for the record: this one arrived correctly flagged as affecting the loop because its permit names the Ted Corbitt Loop outright. The three races the weekend before did not, and had to be caught by name. **Location is the reliable signal; the feed's own tagging is not.**
